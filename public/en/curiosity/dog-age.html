Why is multiplying by 7 wrong?

The "one dog year = 7 human years" rule is an inaccurate simplification. Dogs mature very quickly in their first years — a 1-year-old dog is already sexually mature, equivalent to around 31 human years — and then age more slowly. A 2019 study from the University of California proposed a logarithmic formula based on epigenetic changes in DNA.

Human years = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31

Dog age curiosities

  • Large breeds age faster than small ones. A Great Dane can be "elderly" at 7, while a Chihuahua can reach 18 in good health.
  • The oldest officially recorded dog was Bobi, a Portuguese mastiff that passed away in 2023 aged 31 — although the record was later disputed.
  • The first year of a dog's life equals about 31 human years according to the scientific formula. The second adds another 11.

Frequently asked questions

Why is multiplying by 7 wrong?

Because dogs mature quickly at first and then more slowly. A 1-year-old dog already equals around 31 human years, not 7.

What is the scientific formula?

Human years = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31. Proposed by a UC San Diego study in 2019 based on epigenetic DNA changes.

Do large dogs age faster?

Yes, large breeds generally have shorter lifespans. A Great Dane can be elderly at 7-8, while a Chihuahua can reach 15-18 in good health.
